## Context

**This issue's original premise — "every enrolled Person must enrol again" — is superseded by DL-145
and is no longer canonical.** **DL-145** (accepted 2026-11-06, Issue #208) classifies each Person
affected by a Breaking Identity Update as exactly one of: **automatic regeneration successful**,
**manual re-enrollment required**, or **unaffected**. **OD-87 governs only the household-facing
lifecycle for Persons classified manual-re-enrollment-required.** This ADR does not reopen how that
classification is reached — DL-145 already owns the Option A/B policy, retention of enrollment
recordings, Person consent to retained evidence, the Voiceprint Compatibility Contract, the
compelling-reason gate, Breaking Identity Update classification, administrator acknowledgement,
automatic regeneration, technical regeneration validation, and the safe old/new Voiceprint transition.

**DL-41 already classifies the mechanism** precisely: an incompatible artifact makes a capability
unavailable, the missing/invalid dependency is named, and the outcome is never a trust, permission, or
identity failure. **What had no owner was the household event that follows** — notification, the
interval before re-enrollment, fallback behaviour, consent survival, completion/accountability, and the
relationship to adoption (**OD-85**). This ADR supplies that household event.

## Decision

### 1. Scope: only Persons classified manual-re-enrollment-required

**OD-87 applies only to Persons classified by DL-145 as MANUAL RE-ENROLLMENT REQUIRED.** Persons
automatically regenerated by DL-145 never enter this lifecycle — no manual-re-enrollment Communication,
no degraded-behaviour treatment, no completion tracking. Unaffected Persons (no active Voiceprint,
Voice Identity disabled) require no action. **The original "every enrolled Person must enrol again"
premise is corrected: only the Person-specific subset DL-145 actually classifies this way is affected.**

### 2. Voiceprint-based Identity evidence becomes unavailable — not a failure of any kind

For an affected Person, Voiceprint-based Identity evidence is unavailable until a valid replacement
Voiceprint is created and accepted. This is never represented as: the Person deleted, the Person
absent, the Person untrusted, the Person unauthorized, a Voiceprint mismatch, a fraudulent speaker, a
failed authentication, or a revoked identity (unless consent/revocation independently applies). **DL-41
capability/dependency-outcome semantics apply unchanged.**

> **Canonical principle: VOICEPRINT-BASED IDENTITY UNAVAILABLE ≠ PERSON UNKNOWN IN EVERY CONTEXT ≠
> IDENTITY FAILURE ≠ TRUST FAILURE ≠ PERMISSION FAILURE.**

### 3. Other Identity evidence continues unchanged

The Voiceprint is one Identity evidence source among several (**DL-32**). Its unavailability does not
invalidate any other source — device association, presence evidence, other accepted session/account
evidence, and any other current Identity evidence continue to contribute exactly as before, under the
same **DL-38** Fusion Policy. Neither weakened nor elevated merely because Voiceprint is unavailable.

### 4. Authenticated Companion App remains an independent Identity path

**A Person whose Voiceprint requires re-enrollment may still be sufficiently identified through that
Person's own accepted authenticated Companion App session** — restating
`adr-voiceprint-gallery-structure-under-channel-diversity.md`'s own scope clarification unchanged: voice
comparison is invoked only where the speaker's identity must be established from voice evidence, never
as a redundant check atop an already-sufficient Authenticated Session Identity. **The correct statement
to the Person is never "Your Identity is unavailable"** — it is *"Voice-based Person identification
through shared voice assistants is unavailable."* Operational Trust continues to evaluate what the
authenticated Person may do, exactly as always.

### 5. Transcription remains ungated

Restating **DL-50** unchanged: speaker evidence never gates transcription. A shared voice assistant
continues to detect the wake word, capture the utterance, transcribe it, and classify the requested
capability according to native voice architecture. Voiceprint unavailability affects **Person
resolution only**, never the voice pipeline itself.

### 6. Person-varying outcomes require sufficient current Identity

Where an outcome varies by Person and the available non-Voiceprint evidence does not sufficiently
resolve the speaker, HTBW never guesses the Person — no applying Tom's preference, David's
authorization, another Person's private data, Person-specific Continuity, Person-specific
Communications, or Person-specific capability policy to an unresolved speaker. Current **Unknown
Person** and degradation rules apply exactly as already accepted.

### 7. Generic and non-Person-varying behavior continues per existing capability authorization

Where current governance permits an outcome without a sufficiently resolved Person, generic behavior
may continue — capability-specific, never a universal rule that all shared-voice-assistant
functionality stops, and never a universal rule that every command proceeds regardless. Existing
capability-specific Identity and Operational Trust requirements remain fully authoritative.

### 8. Distinguishing a known migration cause from ordinary `unknown`, without leaking another Person's status

**P15** requires the household-facing cause remain distinguishable from an ordinary `unknown` result,
where authorized and evidenced. Internally, HTBW distinguishes: **(A) speaker not sufficiently
resolved** (insufficient or ambiguous Identity evidence, the ordinary case) from **(B) Voiceprint-based
identification unavailable for an affected Person because manual re-enrollment is required** (a known
compatibility/migration condition). **Critically, the Home may know that one or more household Persons
require re-enrollment without knowing which Person just spoke** — an unidentified speaker is never told
another Person's biometric migration status (*"David needs to re-enroll"*), and the count of affected
Persons is itself protected where current disclosure governance treats it so. An unidentified speaker
receives only a household-safe statement, for example: *"I couldn't identify the speaker,"* *"Voice
identification is currently unavailable for some household members,"* or *"I need another accepted way
to identify you before I can do that."* **"Permission denied" is never stated when the actual condition
is Identity evidence unavailability.**

### 9. Governed Communication to the affected Person

**DL-145 already requires a governed Communication (DL-142) to each Person classified manual-
re-enrollment-required; OD-87 consumes and completes the household lifecycle around it.** The
Communication explains: that Voice Identity technology changed; that the previous Voiceprint is
incompatible/unavailable; that shared voice assistants may not identify the Person until re-enrollment;
that other accepted Identity methods remain available; and the current governed re-enrollment action —
without exposing another Person's biometric state. **No second messaging mechanism is created** —
native Home Assistant Notifications are used where current Communication architecture already calls
for them, and the Person may receive it through an authorized private surface such as the authenticated
Companion App.

**Category: System** (`communication.md`'s existing seven-value enumeration), consuming the existing
"technical health, availability, configuration, integration, platform behaviour... reauthentication
required" definition directly — no new Category is created. **Posture**: actionable; remains outstanding
until resolved or otherwise dispositioned (§13); not automatically Safety/Security-class urgency; does
not produce high-interruption repeated announcements merely because enrollment remains pending.
**Originating/owning responsibility**: Identity originates the Communication (the condition it
describes is Identity's own); Concierge governs delivery, retry, and presentation per its own
already-accepted role (**DL-28**, unchanged).

### 10. Delivery retry is not migration escalation

Delivery retry (**DL-98**) remains ordinary Concierge behaviour, formally distinct from any migration
escalation concept — none is created. Where a reminder/follow-up is required because the action remains
incomplete, current Communication/obligation architecture is reused (an outstanding Communication, its
own re-presentation preference, or an existing Stewardship obligation pattern where one genuinely
applies) — **no unbounded nag loop**, and **the Household Inbox (DL-141) is never turned into a
migration store.**

### 11. Consent survival into re-enrollment

Reconciling **DL-56** and **DL-145** together: **existing Voice Identity consent survives into manual
re-enrollment where its scope and purpose remain unchanged** — the Person's prior consent to "Voice
Identity enrollment/participation" already covers creating and using a replacement Voiceprint under
that same, already-accepted purpose; a Voiceprint recreation is not a new purpose. **Consent must be
reconfirmed** where: the consent scope does not cover the processing now required (for example, a
household's first election of DL-145's Option A retention capability, which is its own distinct
consent dimension the Person has not previously been asked about); the previous consent was withdrawn
or otherwise no longer valid; or another already-accepted condition requires fresh consent. **Current
Person consent state at re-enrollment time is authoritative** — old consent is never treated as
permanently valid irrespective of its current state, and fresh consent is never invented as mandatory
merely because the derived Voiceprint must be recreated when purpose and scope are otherwise unchanged.

### 12. Re-enrollment reuses the existing accepted process

Manual re-enrollment is the Person's existing accepted Voice Identity enrollment process — **no second
"migration enrollment" flow is created.** The Person reaches the governed enrollment interaction;
current consent is confirmed under §11; enrollment samples are captured; the Voice Identity runtime
creates the replacement Voiceprint; the Voiceprint validates; the replacement becomes the Person's one
authoritative Voiceprint (**DL-144** unchanged); source recordings are retained or deleted per the
standing **DL-145** Option A/B policy and the Person's consent; Voiceprint-based Identity becomes
available again.

### 13. Per-Person completion

A Person's manual re-enrollment is complete only when: the replacement Voiceprint was successfully
generated; technical validation passed; the replacement became authoritative; the old incompatible
artifact is no longer active; applicable enrollment-evidence retention/deletion completed under
**DL-145**; required lifecycle/provenance records were written; and the Person's Voiceprint-based
capability is available, or an accepted terminal disposition applies (§14). **Opening the enrollment
flow alone, recording only some samples, and acknowledging the Communication are each, individually,
not completion** — restated explicitly, since the Communication lifecycle and the Identity migration
lifecycle are formally separate (§9; a Person may say *"I understand"* without having re-enrolled).

### 14. Accepted terminal dispositions

A Person's own terminal disposition is one of: a replacement Voiceprint successfully enrolled and
active; Voice Identity intentionally disabled for that Person; the Person withdraws or declines Voice
Identity consent; the Person is removed from the household; or another already-existing governed
terminal state. **No new Person Identity state is invented**, and a Person who intentionally disabled
Voice Identity is never left permanently counted as incomplete.

### 15. Partial household completion is normal

Example: Tom automatically regenerated under DL-145; David completes manual re-enrollment; another
Person's manual re-enrollment remains pending; another Person has Voice Identity disabled. Each Person
progresses independently — the pending Person remains degraded only for Voiceprint-based
identification, the disabled Person requires no action, and **one Person's pending enrollment never
blocks another Person's valid Voice Identity or the household's ordinary operation.**

### 16. Household event completion

The household-scale invalidation/migration event is complete when **every Person in the DL-145-
established affected set has reached an accepted terminal disposition** (§14), adjusted only through
governed lifecycle changes (a later consent withdrawal, a later Person removal). Completion is never
defined as "everyone initially enrolled recorded again," since some Persons regenerate automatically,
disable Voice Identity, withdraw consent, or leave the household. **This reuses the existing household-
scale governed act pattern DL-52 already established for adoption** — no new responsibility, and
completion remains fully explainable.

### 17. Responsibility boundaries

- **Identity** owns: the affected Person/Voiceprint lifecycle state, the compatibility/manual-
  re-enrollment condition, replacement-Voiceprint acceptance, and per-Person completion state.
- **Concierge** owns: governed Communication delivery and retry, and presenting household-facing
  guidance through accepted interaction paths.
- **Operational Trust** owns: whether a current requester/speaker may receive Person-specific
  information or perform an action, and disclosure/presentation decisions.
- **The Voice Identity application/runtime** owns: biometric capture/encoder execution, Voiceprint
  generation, and technical validation (**DL-50**, unchanged).
- **OD-93** owns: interaction-surface placement for household-scale acceptance/review where applicable
  — **not decided here.**

**No Voice Identity responsibility and no migration responsibility are created.**

### 18. Relationship to adoption (OD-85/DL-110)

**OD-87 remains separate from DL-110's adoption decision.** Adoption is initial household acceptance/
coexistence; identity-artifact invalidation and re-enrollment is a later lifecycle migration event. They
share only the interaction-surface-placement question routed to **OD-93**, never the underlying
semantics, authority, or completion model — those remain fully Identity-specific and are not absorbed
into adoption.

### 19. Relationship to OD-93

**OD-87 decides**: what must be communicated, what states/actions exist, what completion means, and
what household consequences apply. **OD-93, now closed as DL-148, decides**: the household-scale
migration acknowledgement/re-enrollment action is surfaced via the governed Communication (§10) leading
to the contextual Person/Voice Identity configuration — never a standalone migration UI, dashboard, or
centralized acceptance queue. See
[adr-contextual-governed-acceptance-placement.md](adr-contextual-governed-acceptance-placement.md).
A household conversational/interrogative question about the migration uses **Assist** (**DL-140**,
unchanged); delivery uses native Home Assistant Notifications per **DL-142**, unchanged.

### 20. Fallback behaviour, stated precisely

No universal fallback exists that impersonates the affected Person. **Sufficient other Identity
evidence** resolves the Person normally, with Operational Trust evaluating the request as always.
**Insufficient Identity where the capability allows an Unknown/generic outcome** applies the existing
**unidentified-person policy** (`operational-trust.md`, unchanged). **Insufficient Identity where a
Person-specific outcome is required** degrades honestly — refuse, or request another accepted
identification path where current architecture permits — **never** assuming the household Owner, the
nearest Person, the Room occupant, the phone owner on a shared endpoint, the last speaker, or reusing a
stale prior Identity Assertion beyond its correct scope (**DL-64**, §21 below).

### 21. Identity Assertion lifetime is unaffected

Consuming **DL-64** unchanged: migration state is never a reason to extend an old Identity Assertion
past its own current-interaction applicability, and a previously resolved Person is never reused
indefinitely across later interactions merely because re-enrollment is inconvenient. Current-interaction
Identity continues to follow the same assertion lifetime/correlation rules as every other interaction.

### 22. Locality is unchanged

Consuming **DL-120** unchanged: manual re-enrollment's biometric processing remains HA-Prod-local only.
No cloud processing is introduced, and Voice Identity application placement is not relaxed or reopened.

### 23. Re-enrollment failure

If replacement enrollment fails: no valid replacement Voiceprint becomes authoritative; Voiceprint-based
Identity remains unavailable; current failure/degradation vocabulary applies (**DL-41**); the
Communication/action remains unresolved per the existing lifecycle; Explainability records the failure
honestly; other Identity mechanisms remain available. **The old incompatible Voiceprint is never
restored as a fallback, and no partially valid Voiceprint authority is ever created** (**DL-144**
unchanged).

### 24. Explainability

Where authorized, Explainability distinguishes: current speaker unresolved; Voiceprint unavailable;
manual re-enrollment required; automatic regeneration succeeded; Voice Identity disabled; consent
withdrawn; replacement enrollment pending; replacement enrollment failed; replacement Voiceprint
validated and active; migration event complete/incomplete (for the household, where authorized); other
Identity evidence available; and an Operational Trust refusal following successful Person resolution,
kept distinct from an Identity-evidence shortfall. **Never exposed**: Voiceprint embeddings, raw
enrollment recordings, another Person's migration state, or other biometric internals — governed
references only.

### 25. Privacy, disclosure, retention, and erasure

An Unknown Person or unidentified speaker never receives another Person's biometric migration status
(§8). Historical truth distinguishes, without reconstructing deleted biometric data: that an artifact
existed; that it became incompatible; that it was removed; that a replacement was created; whether
source evidence was retained or deleted under **DL-145**; and the applicable consent state — consuming
**DL-136** and existing Identity retention rules unchanged. **Incompatible biometric artifacts are never
retained merely for Explainability** where current deletion rules require their removal; a governed
evidence-class/reason-code reference remains sufficient to explain the Home honestly (**P27**).

## Required answers to OD-87's six open questions

1. **Notification.** A Person classified manual-re-enrollment-required receives a governed Communication
   (**DL-142**), originated by Identity, delivered by Concierge, Category **System**, actionable,
   non-emergency posture, never producing unbounded reminder interruptions (§9–§10).
2. **The interval before re-enrollment.** Voiceprint-based Identity evidence is unavailable; other valid
   Identity evidence remains usable; Person-specific outcomes requiring sufficient Identity degrade
   conservatively; transcription continues; the authenticated Companion App remains independently
   usable (§3–§7).
3. **Fallback behaviour.** Sufficient other evidence resolves normally; otherwise capability-specific
   Unknown Person/generic behaviour or honest refusal applies, distinguishing a known migration cause
   from ordinary `unknown` without disclosing an unidentified speaker's — or another Person's — status
   (§8, §20).
4. **Consent.** Existing consent survives where scope/purpose are unchanged; reconfirmation is required
   only where scope changes, prior consent is invalid, or another accepted condition requires it;
   current consent state at re-enrollment time is authoritative (§11).
5. **Completion and accountability.** Per-Person completion requires a validated authoritative
   replacement Voiceprint or another accepted terminal disposition; the household event completes when
   every affected Person reaches a terminal disposition; responsibility boundaries are named (§13–§17).
6. **Relationship to adoption.** OD-87 remains separate from OD-85/DL-110; the household-scale act
   pattern is reused; **OD-93, now closed as DL-148**, places the re-enrollment interaction (§19).
